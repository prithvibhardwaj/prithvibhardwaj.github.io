01 / About

Computer Science and Statistics at NUS.

I moved to Singapore in 2024 to read Computer Science and Statistics at NUS. The two halves fit together well: statistics gives me the instinct to ask whether a number can be trusted, and computer science gives me the tools to ship something that depends on it.

Most of what I have built in the last two years sits where machine learning meets a real user. A medication label scanner that regenerates instructions as pictograms in 13 languages. A deepfake detector that lets you register any Hugging Face model. A nutrition service that turns a photo of lunch into confidence-scored macros. In each case the interesting problem was rarely the model. It was everything around it: validation layers, caching, deployment, and making sure the output could not quietly go wrong.

Outside the editor, I edit. I led a school magazine for four years and still care about writing that says one thing clearly. I also spent two years running events for the Developer Group at NUS Computing, which taught me more about coordination than any module did.

Canon

2026 — now

Extracts decisions, promises, debts and open questions from WhatsApp and Telegram exports, each traceable to the exact message it came from.
